A lot of local business owners set up a Google Business Profile, see it show up in local search results with their hours, reviews, and a map pin, and reasonably wonder whether that's enough — do you actually need a separate website too? The two do different jobs, and most businesses genuinely need both, not one instead of the other.

What a Google Business Profile is good at

It's how you get found. Someone searching "plumber near me" or "cafe open now" sees your listing in the map results before they see much else, and for a quick decision — is this place open, how far away is it, what do reviews say — it does that job well. It's free, it's fast to set up, and it's genuinely useful.

Where it runs out

A Business Profile is a fixed format. You get a few photos, a short description, posts that expire, and reviews you don't fully control the presentation of. There's no room for a proper services list, a gallery of real work, a menu, or a contact form that goes straight to your inbox. It's a listing, not a website — it tells someone you exist and roughly what you do, not why they should pick you over the next listing down.

What a website adds

Depth, and control. The specific services you offer and don't. Photos that show your actual work, not just your storefront. A way for someone to reach out that isn't limited to a phone number or a review comment. And critically, a page you fully own — no algorithm change or policy update can alter how it looks or whether it's shown.

How they work together

In practice, the Business Profile is where people find you, and the website is where they decide. A visitor sees you in the map results, taps through hoping to see a bit more, and if there's nowhere to tap through to, some of them just move to the next listing. Linking your website from your Business Profile — and keeping both up to date — covers both halves of that journey.

If you've got a Business Profile but no website behind it, that's usually the actual gap. You can put one together for free in a sitting.
