It's tempting to think every business website needs the same things: a homepage, an about section, a contact form, maybe a blog. In practice, what actually matters depends a lot on what kind of business it is — and getting that wrong is how you end up with a site that looks fine but doesn't do anything useful.

If you're a trade — electrician, plumber, builder

What sells you is proof of work. Photos of finished jobs matter more than paragraphs of copy. A gallery, clear service areas, and a way for someone to reach you fast (a contact form that actually gets checked) do most of the heavy lifting. You don't need much else.

If you're a cafe or restaurant

Your menu is the page people actually want. If it's buried three clicks deep or only available as a PDF, you've made it harder than it needs to be. Hours, location, and a way to see what's on offer before someone walks in — that's the job.

If you're a gym or studio

Energy matters here more than in most other categories — the site should feel like the space does. Beyond that: class times, pricing, and a clear way to book or get in touch. People decide fast; don't make them dig.

If you're a shop or boutique

Photos do the selling. A signage-led, visual-first layout usually outperforms a wall of text. What you stock and where you are matter more than an "our story" page.

The common thread

None of this requires custom development. It requires picking a layout that actually fits the business, filling in real content, and being able to change it yourself when something changes — new hours, new prices, a new service. That's the whole idea behind Launchly's designs: pick the one that matches your business, not a generic template that happens to have a contact form.
