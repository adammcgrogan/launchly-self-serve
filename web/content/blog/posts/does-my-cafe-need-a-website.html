If you run a cafe or a small restaurant, you've probably had this thought: "everyone already finds us on Instagram and Google Maps, do we really need a website too?" It's a fair question. The honest answer is it depends what you're using the website for — and for most food businesses, there's one job a website does that nothing else quite covers.

The menu is the whole point

When someone's deciding whether to walk in, or whether to book, the thing they actually want is the menu — not your Instagram grid, not a Google Maps listing with three photos of your storefront. A proper page with your actual menu, kept up to date, answers the one question that decides the visit: is there something here I want to eat, and roughly what will it cost me.

A PDF menu buried in a Google Business Profile post from four months ago doesn't do this well. It's slow to load, easy to lose track of, and awkward to update when a dish changes or you run out of something seasonal.

Hours that are actually correct

Third-party listings drift out of date constantly — bank holiday hours, a seasonal closure, a change in your Sunday hours. Your own site is the one place you fully control, so it's the one place that's reliably right, as long as you're the one updating it.

Somewhere to send people

Instagram and Google Maps are where people discover you. A website is where you send people once they already know who you are — in a booking confirmation, on a flyer, in a reply to "do you guys have a website?" Not having an answer to that question, even a simple one, reads as smaller than you are.

So — do you need one?

If your menu changes often, if hours matter (seasonal, holidays, event closures), or if you're tired of "check our Instagram for the menu," yes. If none of that applies and social alone is genuinely working, it can wait. But for most cafes and restaurants, the menu-first case is strong enough on its own.
