A retail website has one job that's different from most other local businesses: it's selling a feeling as much as a product. Someone deciding whether to visit a boutique or a shop is judging it the way they'd judge the shop window — fast, visual, and mostly on instinct. A lot of small retail sites work against that instead of for it.

Too much text before any product

Paragraphs about your story and values have a place, but not above the fold. If someone lands on your site and the first thing they see is three paragraphs of text before a single photo of what you actually sell, most of them won't scroll to find out. Lead with what you stock, not why you stock it.

Photos that don't match the shop

A generic stock photo, or a single dark phone photo of the shopfront, tells a visitor nothing about what it's actually like to walk in. Retail is a visual category — real photos of your products, your displays, your space, do more selling than copy ever will. It's the closest thing to walking past the window when someone's only seeing you on a screen.

Burying what you actually stock

"Homeware and gifts" is vague enough to mean almost anything. If you specialise — a particular style, a specific range of brands, handmade goods, a niche — say so clearly and early. It's also what someone's actually searching for; nobody types "gift shop," they type "handmade candles [town]."

No reason to visit today

A current opening hours listing, a note about new stock, a seasonal range — small, current details signal the shop is active and worth the trip today, not just a listing that's been sitting untouched for a year. It doesn't need to be complicated, just current.
