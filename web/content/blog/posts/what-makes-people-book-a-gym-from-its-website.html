Someone lands on a gym or studio's website already half-decided — they're not browsing for entertainment, they're checking whether this place is worth the trip. What loses them at that stage is almost never the workouts on offer. It's not being able to find the three things they actually came for.

Class times and pricing, not "get in touch for details"

Hiding your schedule or pricing behind a contact form doesn't create urgency, it creates friction. Most people deciding between two gyms will just pick the one where they can actually see what a week looks like and what it costs, rather than message one and wait for a reply. If you run rolling memberships or drop-in rates, say so plainly.

What the space actually looks like

Energy matters more here than in most other local business categories — the site should feel like the space does. Real photos of the space, the equipment, a class in session, do more than any description. People are picturing themselves there before they've committed to a visit, and a stock photo of someone else's gym doesn't help them do that.

A frictionless way to take the first step

Whether that's booking a trial class, claiming an intro offer, or just messaging to ask a question — the site should make that one action obvious and immediate, not the fifth thing on a busy homepage. Someone motivated enough to visit your site is motivated enough to act right now, if you make it easy.

Where people actually go once they've decided

Once someone's chosen your gym over the alternative, your website becomes the reference point — the class schedule they check before showing up, the page they send a friend who's asking where they train. That's worth keeping accurate and current, not something you set up once and forget.
